FOR ADULTS

About the Listening Mission

What is it?

GLORB // Listening Mission is an explicit-teaching interactive lesson. Students help Glorb work out what active listening can look, sound and feel like, connect listening behaviours to the body, and then use those ideas in a conversation with Pip.

What it is for

It makes an often-unwritten social skill more concrete by breaking active listening into observable actions that can be taught, practised and discussed.

Learning focus

Students practise noticing listening behaviours, waiting while another person speaks, showing attention in different ways and choosing a response that connects to what has been said.

Teaching aid, not a real-life test

Completion shows supported performance inside this lesson. It does not prove that a student will use the same listening skills independently across different people, settings or real conversations.

Learning sequence

LEARN → SORT → MAP → APPLY

Learn

Students work through 12 active-listening examples. Each behaviour is named, illustrated and accompanied by an explanation of what it means and why it may help during a conversation.

Sort

Students organise listening examples into LOOKS LIKE, SOUNDS LIKE and FEELS LIKE to make the skill more concrete.

Map

Students connect listening strategies to the head, eyes, ears, mouth, body, hands and feet.

Apply

Students help Glorb choose several listening actions in a conversation with Pip, then choose a response that stays connected to Pip's topic.

Students practise

Recognising active listening, showing attention, managing their conversational turn, waiting for another person to finish, asking relevant questions and responding to what the speaker has said.

Explicit teaching

The mission turns an abstract instruction such as “listen properly” into named, observable actions that can be shown, discussed and practised.

Active listening is more than hearing

The lesson treats listening as an interpersonal process involving attention, processing and response.

From model to application

The social problem comes first, followed by explicit teaching and practice before the learner returns to a similar interaction.

ORIGINAL PROBLEM → LEARNING → PRACTICE → REPAIRED INTERACTION

Formative feedback

Incorrect answers stay inside the learning process. Students receive information that helps them reconsider a choice and try again.

Developmentally respectful design

The skill is taught explicitly while the visual style, humour and interaction design remain age-respectful.

Important

This is an educational practice activity. It is not a clinical, medical or psychological assessment.

Use with in-person teaching

Pair the mission with discussion, adult modelling, role-play and real conversations outside the screen.

Listening can look different

The behaviours in the mission are teaching examples, not a checklist every person must perform in exactly the same way.

Eye contact, body orientation, movement and other visible signs of attention can vary between people.

Support is individual

Focus on whether the learner is able to attend, understand and respond rather than requiring one fixed listening posture.

How to support a student

Let the student try first. Then ask: What showed you that Glorb was listening? What did Glorb do differently? How did his reply connect to what Pip said? Could someone show they are listening in another way?

Interpret completion carefully

The research base supports the teaching and design rationale used here. It does not show that completing this lesson proves real-world listening ability or that GLORB itself has been independently proven effective.
